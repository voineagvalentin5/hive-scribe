What this is

Hive Scribe is a free interlinear Bible study app by Codex Hive Labs (formerly BIBLE SCOL). Logo and app icons: Valentin's Hive Scribe brand art. The whole 73-book Catholic canon plus the Orthodox / Septuagint books (1 Esdras, Prayer of Manasseh, 3 Maccabees, Psalm 151) and the appendix books 4 Maccabees and 2 Esdras, loaded chapter by chapter. Use Books shown in the Library to pick a canon. Labels on each verse show where each piece of data comes from.

Coverage

    Data sources and licenses

    • English, default: King James Version (KJV), with the Apocrypha: the Authorized Version in the standardized text of 1769, with the Apocrypha of the 1611 KJV (Tobit, Judith, the Rest of Esther, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch with the Epistle of Jeremy, the Song of the Three Children, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, the Prayer of Manasses, 1–2 Maccabees, 1–2 Esdras), from eBible.org “eng-kjv” (eng-kjv_vpl.zip), courtesy of the Crosswire Bible Society and eBible.org. eBible.org marks it “Public Domain” and notes: “Letters patent issued by King James with no expiration date means that to print this translation in the United Kingdom or import printed copies into the UK, you need permission … This royal decree has no effect outside of the UK, where this work is firmly in the Public Domain.” Words the translators supplied (italic in printed KJVs, bracketed in the source) are shown in italics. The KJV text is placed on the app's verse numbers (see the WEB below) so the original-language layers stay aligned; where the KJV numbers a verse differently its own reference is shown beside the verse: Romans 16:25–27 (shown at 14:24–26, where the Greek has the doxology), Greek Esther (the Rest of Esther chapters 10–16 placed where the Greek has them), Daniel 3 (the Song of the Three Children) and 13–14 (Susanna, Bel), a few verses of Sirach, and 2 Esdras 7 (the KJV lacks verses 36–105 of the modern text). Placement in those books was done automatically by comparing the KJV with the WEB. 3 and 4 Maccabees and Psalm 151 are not in the KJV, so they are shown in the WEB. Choose the English version in Settings or the reading bar.
    • English, alternative (all books): World English Bible, 2020 stable text, with the deuterocanonical books (eBible.org “eng-web”), public domain. Esther uses the WEB's Greek Esther (with the additions) and Daniel the WEB's Greek Daniel (with the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon), as in Catholic Bibles. Baruch 6 is the Letter of Jeremiah. Chapter and verse numbers in the app follow the WEB (English tradition, essentially the same as the KJV's), so a few differ from the NABRE (e.g. Joel has 3 chapters and Malachi 4).
    • Spanish: RVR 1909 (Español) — Reina-Valera 1909: the classic Spanish Bible, public domain, from eBible.org “spaRV1909” (spaRV1909_vpl.zip; source files dated 10 Aug 2015, packaged 30 Oct 2026). eBible.org marks it “Public Domain / Dominio Público,” translated by Reina y Valera, dialect Castellano 1909. The 66 Protestant books are built into the app and work offline, like the KJV. The text is placed on the app's verse numbers so the original-language layers stay aligned; where the RVR 1909 numbers a verse differently its own reference is shown beside the verse: Romans 16:25–27 (shown at 14:24–26, where the Greek has the doxology), Esther 4:5–6, 9:4–5 and 9:29–30 (combined as in the KJV/WEB grid), and Daniel 3:24–30 (shown at 3:91–97, after the Song of the Three Children). The RVR 1909 does not include the Greek additions to Esther and Daniel, or Daniel 13–14 (Susanna, Bel and the Dragon); those verses note that they are not in the RVR 1909, and deuterocanonical or Orthodox books are shown in the KJV or WEB. Choose it in Settings or the reading bar.
    • Hebrew and Aramaic OT (39 books): STEPBible TAHOT (Translators Amalgamated Hebrew OT), © Tyndale House Cambridge / STEPBible.org, CC BY 4.0, from STEPBible.github.io/STEPBible-Data. Its Hebrew is the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC 4.20, public domain) via OpenScriptures, with Tyndale corrections toward the Leningrad Codex. Morphology is ETCBC parsing written in the OpenScriptures (OSHB) code format; lemmas are extended Strong's numbers; the small English under each word, the transliteration and the Qere/Ketiv marks are TAHOT's. Psalm titles (Hebrew verse 1, or 1–2) are shown with English verse 1, as the WEB prints them. Changes: words regrouped by WEB verse; slash separators removed for display.
    • Greek NT (27 books): SBL Greek New Testament text, © 2010 Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, used under the SBLGNT license; morphology and lemmas from MorphGNT, CC BY-SA 3.0. English word glosses, transliteration and Strong's numbers from STEPBible TAGNT (CC BY 4.0, Tyndale House / STEPBible.org), matched word by word to the SBLGNT text (words TAGNT lists for the SBL edition only). The WEB follows the Majority Text, so a few WEB verses (e.g. John 7:53–8:11, Acts 8:37) have no SBLGNT text.
    • Greek NT, Byzantine Textform (27 books): Maurice A. Robinson and William G. Pierpont, The New Testament in the Original Greek: Byzantine Textform, 2018 edition (RP2018), from the official repository github.com/byztxt/byzantine-majority-text (commit 788ffe7). Its license file states: “THIS REPOSITORY, INCLUDING CODE AND TEXTUAL CONTENTS, IS IN THE PUBLIC DOMAIN” (with the CC0 1.0 dedication). The accented text (CCAT files, main text without the variant apparatus) is paired word for word with Dr. Robinson's Strong's numbers and parsing codes; parsing is shown in Robinson's code system. English word glosses and transliterations are borrowed from STEPBible TAGNT (CC BY 4.0) where the same word appears in the SBLGNT layer; otherwise the TBESG brief gloss and a computed transliteration are used. Where RP's Strong's number differs from TAGNT's tagging of the same word (e.g. κἀγώ, πρῶτον), RP's number is shown and the lexicon follows TAGNT. Verse numbers in RP match the WEB, including Romans 14:24–26 (the doxology). RP has no text at Luke 17:36, Acts 8:37, 15:34 and 24:7, and does not include 1 John 5:7b–8a (the Johannine Comma); it does include Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11. Variant marks: when both Greek NT witnesses are turned on, words that differ between SBLGNT and RP are marked with a dotted underline and a symbol (+ only in this text, ≈ different form or word, ‸ the other text has extra words here). Accents, breathings, capitals and punctuation are not compared.
    • Septuagint Greek (OT and deuterocanonical books): Greek text of Brenton's Septuagint with Apocrypha (eBible.org “grcbrent”), public domain. No openly licensed, morphologically tagged LXX was found: the tagged Rahlfs texts (CCAT-based, e.g. eliranwong/LXX-Rahlfs-1935) are CC BY-NC-SA or carry the CCAT user agreement, so they are not used. The LXX therefore has no tagged word glosses, lemmas or parsing here; tapping a word shows an approximate lexicon match instead (see “Approximate word study” below). Because the Septuagint numbers and orders many chapters differently, each LXX verse was placed next to the WEB verse automatically by comparing the WEB with Brenton's public-domain English translation of the same Greek; the LXX reference is always shown. Placement is least reliable in Jeremiah, 1 Kings (3 Kingdoms), Nehemiah, Proverbs 24–31, Exodus 35–40 and Sirach 30–36; about 2% of OT verses have no LXX match.
    • Orthodox and appendix books (Prayer of Manasseh, 1 Esdras, 3 Maccabees, 4 Maccabees, Psalm 151, 2 Esdras): English from the World English Bible (eBible.org “eng-web”), which states: “The World English Bible is in the Public Domain.” Greek from Brenton's Septuagint (eBible.org “grcbrent”, marked Public Domain by eBible.org) for every book except 2 Esdras, which has no Greek in Brenton (it survives mainly in Latin), so it is English only. Psalm 151's Greek is Brenton's Psalm 151. As for the rest of the Septuagint layer, each Greek verse was placed next to the WEB verse automatically by comparing the WEB with Brenton's public-domain English, and the LXX reference is always shown (e.g. 4 Maccabees 10:4 = LXX 10:3a). Text only: no glosses, lemmas or parsing. The Clementine Vulgate used here has no text for these books. In the Library they are tagged Orth (Orthodox) and App (appendix) and can be hidden with Books shown.
    • Latin Vulgate (all 73 books, where a match was found): Clementine Vulgate Latin text, public domain, from Michael Tweedale’s transcription (vulsearch), via the Open Bibles collection (lat-clementine.usfx.xml). Users are requested to acknowledge that source and to make any modifications clear: the wording of the Latin is unaltered; verses were grouped to the WEB by automatic alignment with the public-domain Douay-Rheims (same collection, eng-dra.usfx.xml). The Vulgate chapter and verse reference is always shown when it differs (for example in the Psalms). Text only: no tagged glosses or parsing; tapping a word shows an approximate match (see “Approximate word study” below).
    • Targum (Aramaic): Targum Onkelos on the Torah (Sefaria “Onkelos Genesis…Deuteronomy”, source toratemetfreeware.com, public domain) and Targum Jonathan on the Prophets, Joshua–Kings and Isaiah–Malachi (Sefaria “Mikraot Gedolot”, public domain), from the Sefaria API. Hebrew verse numbers were converted to English ones with TAHOT's verse map. No Targum is included for the Writings (Ruth, Chronicles, Ezra–Nehemiah, Esther, Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song, Lamentations, Daniel): Sefaria's versions there are not clearly openly licensed or were not checked. Targum text only: no tagged glosses or parsing; tapping a word shows an approximate match (see “Approximate word study” below).
    • Lexicon: STEPBible TBESH (Hebrew/Aramaic) and TBESG (Greek) brief lexicons, CC BY 4.0. Definitions are shortened to about 300 characters. Occurrence counts are computed from the Hebrew and Greek NT texts above (not the LXX or Targum, which are not lemmatized here).
    • Greek NT, Textus Receptus (4 verses only): Luke 17:36, Acts 8:37, Acts 15:34 and Acts 24:7 are in the KJV but in neither the SBLGNT nor RP2018. Their Greek is shown from F. H. A. Scrivener's 1894 Textus Receptus in Dr. Maurice A. Robinson's transcription, from the official repository github.com/byztxt/greektext-scrivener (commit 6049a43), whose README states “License? Public Domain. Copy freely.” The transcription is unaccented, so these verses are shown without accents, converted letter for letter from its Latin-letter encoding. They are marked “Textual Variant: TR”. Where the SBLGNT lacks a verse that RP2018 has (e.g. John 7:53–8:11), the Greek NT card shows the Byzantine text marked “Textual Variant: Byzantine”, so no Greek card is empty.
    • Approximate word study (Septuagint, Targum, Vulgate, TR verses): these texts are not word-tagged, so tapping a word shows an approximate match with a confidence label, never an exact parse. Greek and Aramaic words are compared with spellings that are tagged in the app's own SBLGNT/TAGNT, RP2018 and TAHOT data, then with the TBESG/TBESH headwords after stripping common prefixes and endings (computed in the app; no outside data). Latin words are analysed with the stems, endings and English meanings of William Whitaker's WORDS (DICTLINE.GEN, INFLECTS.LAT, UNIQUES.LAT, from github.com/mk270/whitakers-words, commit fb7fb6f), whose licence reads: “Copyright William A. Whitaker (1936–2010) … Permission is hereby freely given for any and all use of program and data.” Meanings are shortened, and rare non-ecclesiastical entries are left out to keep the download small.
    • Read aloud: uses the speech voices already on your device (Web Speech API); nothing is downloaded or sent anywhere by the app. Voices use modern pronunciation: they are not cantillation, and not a reconstruction of ancient pronunciation. Aramaic is read with a Hebrew voice, and Latin with an Italian voice when no Latin voice is installed.
    • Cross-references: OpenBible.info, CC BY 4.0 (data file dated 2026-11-09). Top 10 per verse by votes, zero and negative votes dropped. OpenBible.info has no data for the deuterocanonical books.
    • Gematria / isopsephy: computed in the app with the standard letter values (Hebrew: final letters same as regular; Greek: accents, breathings and iota subscript ignored after Unicode decomposition).
    • Logos Bridge notes are hand-written study notes, not sourced data.
    • Fonts: Noto Serif and Noto Serif Hebrew (© The Noto Project Authors, SIL Open Font License 1.1), bundled so the app works offline.
    • Hebrew and Aramaic fragments not included: this app does not invent text for sources it does not carry. Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) is known in Hebrew for about two-thirds of the book (Cairo Geniza, Masada, and related finds), but no openly packaged, clearly licensed Hebrew Sirach was included here, so Sirach is Greek (LXX) and Latin only. Tobit is attested in Aramaic and Hebrew fragments from Qumran; those fragments are not in this app. A Greek fragment of the Letter of Jeremiah (Baruch 6) is known as 7Q2; the text here is the Brenton Septuagint (and the Vulgate where aligned), not a transcription of 7Q2. These are notes about what exists, not additional readings.

    Copyright notices (live translations)

    Known limits

    • The full Bible needs the app served over http(s) (it loads chapters on demand). The single-file version contains Genesis 1 and John 1 only.
    • Your view and notes are saved on this device when the browser allows it.
    • Dyslexia reading mode uses wider spacing, a sans-serif face and higher contrast (OpenDyslexic is not bundled).
    • Guided Studies present a named author’s position in original lesson notes; they do not reproduce copyrighted book text. See each study’s source note.
    • Links: the address bar follows what you are reading (for example #gen.1.2?wit=tg&word=H7307 or #conn/john.1.1), so Back and Forward work and a copied link opens the same verse, text and word.

    Typography & reading

    Text size
    Sans-serif, extra letter and word spacing, higher contrast, no italics.
    Bible version
    The Bible text shown in Read, Polyglot, Connections and the word panel. The original-language layers stay on the same verses whichever version you pick.
    Hebrew & Aramaic vowels
    Cantillation marks come with the Westminster Leningrad Codex Hebrew; the Targum texts have vowels but no cantillation. Consonants only also hides the vowels.
    Canon
    For finer control (Old Testament, New Testament, deuterocanonical books, Greek additions to Esther and Daniel) and quick presets, use Books shown at the top of the Library.
    When this is off (the default), you get a 66-book Protestant view, the same books as the KJV: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees, and the Greek additions to Esther and Daniel are hidden from the picker, chapter nav, lexicon hits and cross-references. When it is on, the app uses the 73-book Catholic canon.
    Original-language witnesses
    Turn a witness off to hide it from the chapter chips and the Polyglot matrix. The Bible text (version chosen above) is always shown.
    Offline
    Chapters you open are kept for offline use.

    Choose book & chapter

    Tap a book, then a chapter. The current chapter is gold. Under each book: which original-language texts it has.
